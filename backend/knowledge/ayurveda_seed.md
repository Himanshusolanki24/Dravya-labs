## Dosha overview

Vata is associated with movement, dryness, and irregularity. Grounding, warm, oily, and regular routines typically pacify Vata. Cold, raw, and erratic schedules often aggravate it.

Pitta is associated with heat, transformation, and intensity. Cooling, mild, and moderately scheduled routines typically pacify Pitta. Excess spice, alcohol, and midday overheating often aggravate it.

Kapha is associated with structure, lubrication, and stability. Light, warm, dry, and stimulating routines typically pacify Kapha. Heavy, sweet, oily foods and inactivity often aggravate it.

## Safety first

Dravya guidance is educational Ayurvedic wellness information. It is not a medical diagnosis, prescription, or emergency service. Users with chest pain, difficulty breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, pregnancy complications, or rapidly worsening symptoms should seek licensed medical care.

Herbs can interact with medications. Do not recommend high-dose herbs for pregnancy, breastfeeding, or severe liver or kidney disease without a clinician.

## Common herb cautions

Ashwagandha may be unsuitable in hyperthyroidism or pregnancy. Turmeric/curcumin can increase bleeding risk with anticoagulants. Licorice can raise blood pressure. Senna and harsh purgatives are not first-line home advice.

## Diet principles

Match meals to the dominant dosha and current imbalance. Prefer freshly cooked food, adequate hydration, and meals at regular times. Respect reported allergies and the user's diet type (vegetarian, vegan, omnivore).

## Lifestyle

Sleep, stress, and daily rhythm strongly influence dosha balance. Gentle yoga, pranayama, and walking are generally safer first recommendations than intense fasting or panchakarma at home.
